Tomato Vegetable & Pinto Bean Stew
==================================

A big-pot, plant-forward stew built for minimal fuss --
sear the eggplant and mushrooms for depth,
then everything else goes in for one long simmer.
Cooking to a full simmer is also the food-safety step.

Ingredients
-----------

* 45 grams olive oil (about 30 grams for the mushrooms, 15 grams for the eggplant)
* 550 grams eggplant (1 large), cut into 2.5 cm cubes
* 225 grams mushrooms, sliced
* 300 grams onion (1 large), diced
* 20 grams garlic (6 cloves), minced
* 200 grams carrots (3), sliced
* 450 grams green cabbage (about half a head), shredded
* 400 grams zucchini (2), sliced
* 225 grams green beans, trimmed and halved
* 1600 grams crushed tomatoes
* 1050 grams drained cooked pinto beans (4 cans), drained and rinsed
* 18 grams salt, plus more to taste
* 1 lemon, for finishing

Method
------

* Chop everything before the pan is hot,
  since the saute moves fast once it starts.
  Cube the eggplant, slice the mushrooms,
  dice the onion, mince the garlic, slice the carrots,
  shred the cabbage, slice the zucchini,
  and trim and halve the green beans.
  Drain and rinse the pinto beans and set them aside.
* Sear the mushrooms:
  heat 30 grams of the olive oil in a wide pan over medium-high
  until shimmering.
  Add the mushrooms in a single layer,
  working in batches if needed so the pan stays crowded but not piled --
  too many at once and they steam pale instead of browning.
  Leave them undisturbed a minute or two to color,
  then stir and cook until browned
  and their released water has cooked off.
  Tip them into your big pot.
* Sear the eggplant:
  add the remaining 15 grams olive oil to the same pan over medium-high.
  Add the eggplant and cook, stirring occasionally,
  until the cubes are browned on some faces
  and collapsing from spongy to silky, about 6-8 minutes.
  Add to the pot with the mushrooms.
* Deglaze the pan:
  with the pan still hot and empty,
  pour in a splash of the crushed tomatoes (or a little water).
  Scrape the browned fond off the bottom as it loosens --
  the acidity lifts it fast.
  Pour that into the pot so the browned flavor rides along.
* Everything in the pot:
  add the onion, garlic, carrots, cabbage, zucchini, green beans,
  the rest of the crushed tomatoes, the pinto beans,
  and the salt to the pot with the seared vegetables.
  Stir to combine.
  It will look overpacked --
  the cabbage and eggplant collapse quickly and make room.
* Simmer, phase 1 (covered, about 20 minutes):
  bring up on medium with the lid on until steam escapes,
  spin the lid to form the vapor seal,
  then drop to low.
  Everything softens and melds, no water lost.
  Keep it at a genuine simmer, not just a hold --
  that is what carries every vegetable
  well past the temperature that makes the pot safe, with margin.
* Simmer, phase 2 (uncovered or lid well ajar, about 20-25 minutes):
  this is where it reduces and the sauce concentrates.
  Stir occasionally -- thick tomato scorches on the base.
  Judge the end by consistency, not the clock:
  pull it when the sauce coats rather than pools.
  Add a splash of water only if it tightens too much.
* Salt and finish:
  taste.
  The long reduction concentrates as water evaporates,
  so it likely needs more salt than it did at the start --
  adjust now.
  If it tastes flat or one-dimensional,
  a squeeze of lemon brightens it.
  Serve.

Notes
-----

The sear on the mushrooms and eggplant is doing flavor work,
not safety work --
it is fine that a hard sear leaves the interiors under temperature,
because the roughly 45-minute two-phase simmer is the kill step
and clears 70C throughout with room to spare.

Induction temperature:
the dial reads the pot bottom through the glass,
and the bottom always runs hotter than the liquid,
so the number you set sits above the stew's actual temperature.
A simmer is 85-96C;
to land there, set the target around 99-104C and adjust.
Easier gauge: calibrate to what you see, not the dial --
small pinprick bubbles rising and occasionally breaking the surface
(phase 2),
or a freely spinning sealed lid (phase 1).
A full 6-liter pot will not hold a simmer at the very bottom setting,
so expect low-medium.

Portioning:
a main-dish serving is about 350 grams --
denser after the reduction, and the beans are filling,
so no need to go higher.
Serve over 60-100 grams cooked rice or pasta,
about 17-28 grams of carbohydrate.
Pasta, cooked al dente, is the marginally gentler base
on the glucose curve than white rice.
Everything going in at once means the zucchini and green beans
cook soft rather than staying firm; that is by design here.
The pintos will soften toward creamy over the full simmer
and thicken the sauce, which is part of the plan.
Better on day two.
